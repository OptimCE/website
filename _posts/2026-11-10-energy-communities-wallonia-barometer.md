As of 10 November 2026, the CWaPE has authorised **twelve energy-sharing activities** within energy communities in Wallonia. They belong to **six of the thirteen communities** with a complete file, and a single community, ES2, runs seven of them. Since July 2024, the Walloon regulator has published **nineteen decisions** on the subject: twelve authorisations and seven modifications. No refusal or withdrawal has been published.

The decisions are public but scattered: each is a PDF announced in the CWaPE's register, among more than 1,500 publications, and the regulator's own list of authorised activities says nothing about participants or conditions. This page brings the nineteen decisions together, counts them and sorts them. It is updated every quarter, at the same address, and its data can be downloaded as a CSV file.

The procedure is covered in [“Create an energy community in Wallonia”](/en/guides/create-energy-community-wallonia/), the forms and deadlines in [“Energy community: CWaPE documents and deadlines”](/en/guides/energy-community-administrative-documents-wallonia/), and the differences between the legal forms in [“Energy communities in Belgium: CER, CEC, CEL”](/en/guides/energy-communities-belgium/). This page counts what the regulator has actually authorised.

One point of vocabulary first, because the whole count depends on it. In Wallonia, an energy community is **notified** to the CWaPE, which records that its file is complete. Each of its **sharing activities** must then be **authorised** by the CWaPE, on a request filed with the distribution system operator (DSO), which gives a technical opinion. A community can therefore exist without sharing anything, and one community can run several activities. This barometer counts the activities.

## What the figures show

### One community runs more than half of all sharing

ES2 holds seven of the twelve authorised activities, all organised around businesses, and five of the seven modifications. According to the decisions, the participants are businesses only in four cases (Energilia Wallonie, Waldorado, Zoning de Hannut, Centracar) and one business plus one private individual in two others (Facq Wallonie, Novandi Wallonie); in the seventh, the only installation named belongs to the Brasserie du Bocq brewery. CEFA-AP1, the activity of Energy For All, also brings together four businesses, joined in May 2026 by five more, three of them large companies.

In the initial authorisations, the decisions mention private individuals in only four activities out of twelve, never more than three at a time: Soleil d'Aubange, Energie Libre, Facq Wallonie and Novandi Wallonie. The Energie Libre modification of March 2026 adds 45 members, 15 of them legal entities. The largest activity, Lasnenergie's, has 65 participants, more than half of the 106 stated in the initial authorisations. Its decision does not say who they are, but the statutes restrict membership to private individuals, local authorities and small businesses based in the municipality of Lasne. United Wallonie's decision does not state the category of its five participants either.

Only one decision names a municipality among the participants: the town of Aubange, which makes the surplus of its installation available. This case is analysed in [“Energy communities: a guide for municipalities”](/en/solutions/municipalities/).

One last thing in common: no community shares its own production. Every decision describes installations held by members for their own use, who share their surplus, and the CWaPE's evaluation report confirms this for all active communities.

### Notifying is not sharing

Seven of the thirteen communities with a complete file had no authorised activity on 10 November 2026. For the other six, the gap between the completion of the file and the first authorisation ranges from 44 days (Soleil d'Aubange) to 374 days (United Wallonie). The guide to [CWaPE documents and deadlines](/en/guides/energy-community-administrative-documents-wallonia/) explains why a community can exist legally without sharing a single kilowatt-hour.

### A quarter between request and decision

The authorisation request is filed with the DSO. The DSO has ten working days to check that the file is complete, then twenty to send its technical opinion to the CWaPE. The CWaPE then decides within forty working days, a period that starts again when it asks for more information.

Across the eleven decisions whose request date is unambiguous, between 43 and 308 days passed from request to decision, with a median of 87 days. The two longest, Waldorado (147 days) and United Wallonie (308 days), are the ones where the CWaPE sent observations to the community before deciding. A project developer should therefore plan in quarters, not weeks.

### Authorised does not mean in operation

Four initial authorisations out of twelve, and two modifications out of seven, were granted with a suspensive condition. The condition always concerns metering: switching every participant to the R3 regime, which sends the quarter-hourly load curve. Twice it also requires the injection to be marketed, and once an installation to be commissioned. Sharing only starts once the condition is met. The guide to [smart meters in Wallonia](/en/guides/smart-meter-wallonia/) explains how to request R3.

The CWaPE makes the distinction itself. Its evaluation report of 30 September 2026 counts nine activities “implemented” within communities, two in CERs and seven in CECs, with 208 offtake points. At that date, twelve had been authorised. The report does not say which ones are running.

### Same-building sharing is ahead of communities

Sharing within a single building does not go through the CWaPE: it is notified to the DSO, so it is not in the tables above. The regulator does publish the total: eighteen same-building schemes were in operation on 30 September 2026, with 129 offtake points. That is twice as many schemes as in communities, with fewer participants. Peer-to-peer sharing remains at zero: the decree provides for it, but the order that should set its rules has not been adopted.

### Elsewhere in Belgium

In Brussels, the regulator Brugel does not authorise each activity but the community itself, for ten renewable years. Its list has 36 authorised communities, the first on 16 May 2023 and the latest on 17 September 2026. In Flanders, communities are not authorised but notified to the regulator, the Vlaamse Nutsregulator (formerly VREG), which counted 80 on 1 September 2025; sharing is then registered with Fluvius. These three figures do not measure the same thing and cannot be added up. For what each regime allows a household to do in practice, see [“Cheaper electricity without switching supplier”](/en/guides/cheaper-electricity-without-switching-supplier/).

## Method and limits

### What the barometer counts

One row per CWaPE decision that authorises or modifies an energy-sharing activity of an energy community. These decisions rest on article 35quaterdecies, § 3, of the Walloon electricity decree of 12 April 2001 and on articles 20 and 21 of the Walloon Government order of 17 March 2023. Sharing within a single building, which only needs a notification to the DSO, and peer-to-peer sharing, which is not yet operational, are excluded.

### How it is compiled

Three CWaPE sources were cross-checked on 10 November 2026:

- the register of recent documents, read page by page;
- the list of authorised sharing activities, which shows the same twelve activities and seven modifications;
- the list of energy communities with a complete file.

Each decision was then read in its PDF. Only what it establishes is used. A field it leaves blank stays empty in the CSV and reads “not stated” in the tables. The 2025 decisions also match the annex of the CWaPE's 2025 annual report.

### What the decisions say, and what they leave out

They give the community and its company number, the activity, the participants' DSOs and number, the installations made available, the DSO's opinion and the conditions. They give neither the peak power in kilowatt-peak, nor the start date, nor the price of a shared kilowatt-hour, nor the details of the allocation key. We reproduce no private individual's name.

### Discrepancies found

- **CD-26a15-CWaPE-1232** (first modification of Energilia Wallonie): the published PDF is the initial authorisation. Later decisions confirm the date, not the content.
- **CD-26e13-CWaPE-1262**: the CWaPE page shows CD-26d23; we follow the PDF, which later decisions also cite.
- **Centracar**: the PDF reads CD-26i03-CWaPE-1231, the page and the list of activities 1291, which we use.
- **Two request dates** for Zoning de Hannut (8 October or 26 June 2025) and for the Energie Libre modification (3 or 7 July 2025). Zoning de Hannut is left out of the time calculation.

### Updates

Every quarter, the count is redone the same way; the page and the CSV keep their address, and a line is added to the update history. Spotted an error or a missing decision? Write to [contact@optimce.be](mailto:contact@optimce.be).

## The data as CSV

The file [energy-sharing-authorizations-wallonia.csv](/assets/data/energy-sharing-authorizations-wallonia.csv) has one row per decision, nineteen as of 10 November 2026, in UTF-8 with a comma separator. It is published under the [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence (CC BY 4.0)](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/): you may reuse it, including for commercial purposes, by crediting “OptimCE, based on decisions published by the CWaPE”. The decisions themselves remain CWaPE documents. Notes inside the file are in French, like the decisions.

The columns:

- `activity_id` links an authorisation to its modifications (A01 to A12);
- `decision_ref`, `decision_date`, `publication_date`, `request_date`: reference and dates of the decision, its announcement and the request;
- `decision_type`, `modification_rank`: `authorization` or `modification`, and the modification's rank;
- `community_name`, `community_type`, `community_bce`, `activity_name`: community, CER or CEC, company number and activity name;
- `dso_request`, `dso_participants`, `municipality`: DSO of the request, participants' DSOs (separated by a vertical bar) and municipality of the perimeter;
- `participants`, `participants_added`, `suspensive_condition`: number authorised or added, and suspensive condition (`yes` or `no`);
- `decision_url`, `pdf_url`, `notes`: decision page and PDF, and reading notes.

## FAQ

### How many energy communities are there in Wallonia?

Thirteen with a complete file as of 10 November 2026, according to the CWaPE: seven CERs and six CECs, with 222 participants. Six of them have at least one authorised sharing activity, twelve in total.

### Where can I find the list of energy communities in Wallonia?

The CWaPE publishes the list of communities with a complete file and, separately, the list of authorised activities, without participants or conditions. This barometer combines both with the content of the nineteen decisions, as a table and as a CSV file.

### How does the CWaPE authorise energy sharing?

The request is filed with the DSO, which gives a technical opinion; the CWaPE then decides within forty working days. Any change follows the same procedure. The observed median between request and decision is 87 days.

### What are some examples of energy communities in Wallonia?

Soleil d'Aubange, Lasnenergie with its 65 participants, ES2 with its seven business activities, Energie Libre, Energy For All and United Wallonie: these are the six communities sharing as of 10 November 2026.

### Is sharing within a single building included in this barometer?

No: it is notified to the DSO, without a CWaPE decision. The regulator counted eighteen such schemes in operation on 30 September 2026. Peer-to-peer sharing is not yet operational.

### How can I join one of the energy communities listed?

By contacting the community, which then asks for its activity to be modified. You need a smart meter on the R3 regime and, in a CER, a supply point inside the perimeter. [“Join an energy community in Wallonia”](/en/guides/join-energy-community-wallonia/) explains the steps.
